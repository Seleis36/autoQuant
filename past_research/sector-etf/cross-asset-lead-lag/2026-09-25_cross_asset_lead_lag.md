# cross-asset-lead-lag — do sectors absorb their drivers' moves with a delay?

**Status: Failed.** Asset class `sector-etf`. Date 2026-09-25.

## Idea
Gradual information diffusion: Hong, Torous & Valkanov (2007) find that industries lead the
market; Driesprong, Jacobsen & Maat (2008) that oil moves predict equity returns with a lag;
Rapach, Strauss, Tu & Zhou (2019) that lagged industry returns predict other industries, and
build a LASSO long/short industry book on that. Sector ETFs have obvious fundamental drivers
priced every day in other markets — crude and gas for XLE, copper, gold and the dollar for
XLB/XLI, the 10y yield and curve slope for XLF/XLU/XLRE — so a slow sector would be
predictable from its driver's recent move, net of the market.

## Setup
Common sector-etf setup (IS through 2014 / OOS 2015-2026, 2 bp/side, close *t* → *t+1*,
SPY hedge to beta 0). Target: next-*h*-day **beta-residual** return of each sector.
Drivers: CL=F, HG=F, GC=F, NG=F, DX-Y.NYB, Δ^TNX, Δ(^TNX−^IRX), SPY and all 11 sector
residuals, over past 1/5/21 days. **Futures, dollar index and yields are lagged one extra day**:
they do not close at 16:00 ET with the ETFs, and a same-day value would leak up to an hour of the
ETF's next-day move. Futures history starts 2000-08, so this idea's IS is 2000-09 → 2014.

## Results
### 1. Screen (`screen.py`, 864 sector × driver × horizon × lag tests)
10.6 % of IS tests have |t|>2 (≈5 % by chance) — there is structure — but the *economically
paired* relations do not hold up: copper→XLI +3.3 IS / −1.2 OOS, slope→XLF +3.5 / +1.2,
gold→XLF +4.9 / −2.0, y10→XLU −2.0 / −0.4. The same-sign survivors are mostly cross-sector
residual pairs (XLP→XLB +4.0 / +2.9, XLU→XLY +2.9 / +2.8) — cross-sectional reversal in
disguise (residuals sum to ≈0, so one sector's up-day is the others' down-day), already
covered by `sector-short-reversal`. The one paired relation with a consistent sign was
**oil 5d → XLE residual, −3.3 IS / −2.1 OOS** (XLE over-reacts to crude).

### 2. Walk-forward ridge, Rapach-style (`ridge_wf.py`, 18 cells)
One ridge per sector on all 57 standardised features, re-fit every month end on data up to
*t−h* (expanding or 5-year rolling), λ ∈ {0.1, 1, 10} × n, *h* ∈ {1, 5, 21}; forecasts strictly
out of sample → linear cross-sectional weights, *h*-day tranches.

| Cell | Sharpe full | IS | OOS | OOS gross | forecast IC IS / OOS | turnover |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| h1 λ10 5y (best) | 0.19 | 0.30 | 0.08 | 0.53 | 0.034 / 0.028 | 151×/yr |
| h21 λ10 5y | 0.11 | 0.12 | 0.10 | 0.12 | 0.042 / 0.018 | 6×/yr |
| h5 λ10 5y | 0.06 | 0.16 | −0.03 | 0.06 | 0.036 / 0.023 | 29×/yr |
| typical λ≤1 cell | −0.2…−0.5 | | | | ≈0 | |

Only the heaviest shrinkage survives, i.e. the model does best when it is closest to
"predict nothing". The daily model's small gross edge (0.53 OOS) is eaten by 150× turnover.

### 3. Oil → XLE over-reaction, fixed single-pair book (`oil_xle.py`, 3 cells)
Short XLE-vs-SPY after a 5-day crude rally (and vice versa), 5-day tranches: 0.08 full,
0.31 IS, **−0.16 OOS**. XLE's own residual reversal is worse (−0.53 OOS); oil adds nothing
tradeable.

## Verdict
~21 books on top of an 864-test screen. **No tradeable lead-lag between sectors and their
drivers at daily-to-monthly horizons since 2000**: the relations found in the screen are the
expected false positives of a large search plus cross-sectional reversal, and the
walk-forward model that honestly learns them has OOS IC ≈0.02. Binding constraint: **signal
strength** — ETF-era sector prices incorporate commodity and rate moves the same day.
